# Dual Progression System

## Overview

Geo Legends features a **dual-track progression system** that allows players to choose between **Athletic** (sports-based) and **Martial** (combat-based) advancement paths. Both tracks are viable, but the design encourages Athletic progression through better rewards and lower risk.

## Athletic Track (Encouraged)

### Core Philosophy
- **Non-destructive competition** through sports and skill-based challenges
- **Prestige-focused** progression with cosmetic and social rewards
- **Lower risk, steady rewards** for consistent players
- **Community building** through team sports and regional competitions

### Progression Mechanics
- **Team Training**: Build and train sports teams at your Arena
- **Competition Participation**: Join local, regional, and global tournaments
- **Skill Development**: Improve team ratings through practice and matches
- **Prestige Accumulation**: Earn prestige points for victories and achievements

### Rewards & Benefits
- **Cosmetics**: Team kits, banners, emblems, base decorations
- **Economy Buffs**: Minor production bonuses after victories
- **Social Status**: Titles, leaderboard positions, recognition
- **Faster Civic Growth**: Reduced costs and time for settlement upgrades
- **Lower Risk**: No resource loss from defeats

### Competition Types
- **Local Matches**: Friendly challenges within metro regions
- **Regional Cups**: Weekly tournaments with brackets
- **Leagues**: Persistent ladder systems with ratings
- **Championships**: Seasonal events with major rewards

### Sports Modes
- **Football**: Penalty shootouts, time trials, skill challenges
- **Archery**: Precision and accuracy competitions
- **Racing**: Speed and endurance challenges
- **Orienteering**: Navigation and exploration contests

## Martial Track (Allowed)

### Core Philosophy
- **Territorial control** through strategic combat and raids
- **Resource acquisition** through successful attacks
- **High risk, high reward** gameplay for aggressive players
- **Strategic depth** through unit management and tactics

### Progression Mechanics
- **Unit Training**: Build and upgrade military forces
- **Raid Planning**: Organize attacks on enemy settlements
- **Territory Control**: Capture and defend strategic locations
- **Resource Raiding**: Steal resources from defeated enemies

### Rewards & Benefits
- **Resource Gains**: Direct loot from successful raids
- **Territorial Expansion**: Control more cells and influence
- **Military Prestige**: Recognition for combat prowess
- **Strategic Advantages**: Better positioning for future conflicts

### Combat Systems
- **Autoresolve**: Server-calculated outcomes based on stats
- **Tactical Minigames**: Optional skill-based combat resolution
- **Formation Combat**: Strategic unit positioning and tactics
- **Siege Warfare**: Advanced fortification and siege engine mechanics

### Risk Factors
- **Resource Loss**: Potential losses from failed attacks
- **Unit Casualties**: Military units can be wounded or lost
- **Cooldown Periods**: Time restrictions between raids
- **Retaliation**: Risk of counter-attacks from enemies

## Track Comparison

| Aspect | Athletic Track | Martial Track |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| **Risk Level** | Low | High |
| **Resource Impact** | Positive only | Can lose resources |
| **Time Investment** | Moderate | High |
| **Social Aspect** | High (team sports) | Medium (alliances) |
| **Progression Speed** | Steady | Variable |
| **Reward Type** | Prestige, Cosmetics | Resources, Territory |
| **Skill Requirement** | Reflex, Timing | Strategy, Planning |

## Cross-Track Benefits

### Shared Progression
- **Settlement Level**: Both tracks contribute to overall base advancement
- **Technology Research**: Shared tech trees benefit both paths
- **Resource Production**: Base economy supports both tracks
- **Commander NPCs**: Can be assigned to either track

### Hybrid Strategies
- **Balanced Approach**: Players can pursue both tracks simultaneously
- **Seasonal Focus**: Switch emphasis based on events and opportunities
- **Risk Management**: Use Athletic track for steady progress, Martial for big gains
- **Social Dynamics**: Athletic for community building, Martial for territorial control

## Progression Milestones

### Early Game (Village Era)
- **Athletic**: Build first Arena, train basic team
- **Martial**: Construct Barracks, train first soldiers
- **Shared**: Establish resource production, basic technology

### Mid Game (Township Era)
- **Athletic**: Join regional competitions, improve team ratings
- **Martial**: Plan coordinated raids, expand territory
- **Shared**: Advanced buildings, complex production chains

### Late Game (City Era)
- **Athletic**: Championship participation, prestige leadership
- **Martial**: Major territorial control, advanced warfare
- **Shared**: Era-defining projects, regional dominance

## Player Choice & Agency

### Decision Points
- **Track Selection**: Choose primary focus early in game
- **Resource Allocation**: Balance between tracks
- **Risk Tolerance**: Athletic for safety, Martial for aggression
- **Social Preference**: Team sports vs. strategic warfare

### Flexibility
- **Track Switching**: Can change focus over time
- **Hybrid Play**: Pursue both tracks simultaneously
- **Seasonal Adaptation**: Adjust strategy based on events
- **Personal Style**: Match gameplay to player preferences

## Balance Considerations

### Athletic Track Advantages
- **Lower barrier to entry** for new players
- **Consistent progression** without major setbacks
- **Social engagement** through team activities
- **Cosmetic rewards** for personalization

### Martial Track Advantages
- **Higher potential rewards** for successful players
- **Strategic depth** for experienced gamers
- **Territorial control** for competitive players
- **Resource acquisition** for economic players

### Risk/Reward Balance
- **Athletic**: Steady, predictable progression
- **Martial**: Variable, high-stakes gameplay
- **Both tracks viable** for different player types
- **No forced specialization** - players can choose their path
